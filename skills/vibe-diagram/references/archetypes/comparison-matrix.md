# Archetype: Comparison Matrix

Use when the reader must compare conditions, candidates, current/target behavior, or requirement/evidence differences.

## Recognizable shape

- Conditions or dimensions form one axis; candidates or compared paths form the other.
- Every cell contains a factual value, difference, status, or explicit unknown.
- Important differences are visually highlighted in the matrix itself.
- A conclusion and reconsideration conditions sit next to the comparison.
- Weights and totals appear only when the user supplied or approved them.

## Avoid

- candidate cards with decorative arrows around them;
- invented scores;
- hiding the decisive row in details;
- using a matrix when order or causality is the actual question.

## Product-manager reading

Use product conditions and observable behavior as rows, show the real differences in place, and state the conclusion plus the conditions that could change it. Exact implementation mechanisms remain secondary values or mapped evidence.
